# Source, version, and reproduction evidence

Checked October 10, 2026. Scope: faithful lyric sheets for U.S. pack use, from identified published versions. This is a lyric packet, not a transcription of piano accompaniments or a collection of recordings.

## Reproduction basis

The [U.S. Copyright Office's Circular 15A](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ15a.pdf), page 2, explains the maximum 95-year term for these published works and confirms that works published in the United States before January 1, 1931 are public domain. Each selected text is evidenced in a U.S. publication dated 1881, 1915, or 1927. Even assuming full renewal, those editions' terms expired by the end of 1976, 2010, and 2022 respectively. This conclusion concerns these historical versions in the United States; it does not grant rights in later added verses, arrangements, recordings, or editions, or assert worldwide public-domain status.

Only historical lyrics are transcribed. Modern source-site editorial prose, arrangements, audio, and later lyric variants are not reproduced. The retained scans and source transcriptions below provide the version-specific evidence; the repo's general software license does not alter the underlying songs' public-domain status.

- Retained [page image](source-pages/row-your-boat-1881-p69.png) was visually checked, including all four entry marks. [Bibliographic fields](franklin-bibliography.json) were captured from the archive item's metadata.

### My Bonnie and Scotland's Burning

- [University of Pennsylvania library transcription and scans](https://digital.library.upenn.edu/women/aitch/songs/songs.html): [My Bonnie](https://digital.library.upenn.edu/women/aitch/songs/songs.html#My), [Scotland's Burning](https://digital.library.upenn.edu/women/aitch/songs/songs.html#Scotland).
- Retained original page images: [p. 43](source-pages/my-bonnie-1915-p43.jpeg) and [p. 55](source-pages/scotlands-burning-1915-p55.jpeg). The library's **1915 title/copyright transcription** establishes the chosen edition; its [retained text excerpt](source-pages/golden-book-1915-title.txt) identifies the tenth edition and Chicago publisher. The separate title-page JPEG timed out, so no title-page scan is claimed. The website cover alone is not used as edition evidence.
- Both lyrics were checked against the printed music, not solely the web transcription. Syllabic music hyphens are joined into words; curly apostrophes are represented as straight apostrophes. Punctuation follows the score, with phrase-ending punctuation made readable in the lyric-only layout. No words, word order, or repetitions are changed.
- The optional B-word movement is an established teaching convention, corroborated by [Irish Girl Guides' Trefoil News, Autumn 2018](https://irishgirlguides.ie/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Trefoil-News-Autumn-2018.pdf) and its [Online Scout Manager activity](https://www.onlinescoutmanager.co.uk/programme.php?action=view&id=57835). Its mechanics are paraphrased in one short leader note. Those modern lyrics are not the transcription source. The action applies to every B-word in the retained verses and chorus, including blow, blown, bed, and bro't.

## Candidate decisions

- **Kookaburra**: excluded. [Current licensing information](https://www.easysong.com/search/songs/song-copyright-holder-information.aspx?s=1012401) identifies Marion Sinclair and Music Sales on behalf of Larrikin Music. [Federal Court of Australia discussion of the litigation](https://www.fedcourt.gov.au/digital-law-library/judges-speeches/speeches-former-judges/justice-edelman/edelman-j-20161019) corroborates the rights dispute. No verified public-domain edition or reproduction permission was obtained. No lyrics or replacement song were generated.
- **Bring Back My Bonnie to Me**, H. J. Fulmer, 1882: public-domain age, but rejected as the source for the familiar camp song. The [published sheet and transcription](https://www.sheetmusicsinger.com/bring-back-my-bonnie-to-me/) contain different verses and a different chorus. Its publication date cannot authenticate familiar modern lyrics. The complete 1915 version above was selected instead.
- **Home on the Range**: considered through [Lomax's 1910 collection](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/21300/21300-h/21300-h.htm) and [Library of Congress history](https://www.loc.gov/collections/songs-of-america/articles-and-essays/articles-about-songs/home-on-the-range/). The other choices provide a bounded set of shorter teaching songs without making a shortened composite of its many historical stanzas.

## Fidelity record

[source-transcriptions.json](source-transcriptions.json) contains the manually scan-checked lyric text, exact block order, and separately authored flow/teaching directions. The builder compares each HTML lyric block against that record, then checks that the exported PDF contains each complete block in order. Automatic comparisons protect against later drift; the scan comparisons establish source fidelity. Neither check relies on invented continuation verses.

The sheets' four-phrase rounds use the source's entry interval. The last-pass cue is an ordinary leader instruction for ending the round, not a new lyric or a mandated historical repeat count. No key or fixed tempo is imposed. The source scores retained here are available for leaders who need the melody.
