# Source, version, and reproduction evidence

Checked October 10, 2026. Scope: faithful lyric sheets for U.S. pack use, from identified published versions. This is a lyric packet, not a transcription of piano accompaniments or a collection of recordings.

## Reproduction basis

The [U.S. Copyright Office's Circular 15A](https://www.copyright.gov/circs/circ15a.pdf), page 2, explains the maximum 95-year term for these published works and confirms that works published in the United States before January 1, 1931 are public domain. The initial collection's texts are evidenced in U.S. publications dated 1881, 1915, or 1927. Even assuming full renewal, those editions' terms expired by the end of 1976, 2010, and 2022 respectively. Row and Mountain remain in the active packet. This conclusion concerns these historical versions in the United States; it does not grant rights in later added verses, arrangements, recordings, or editions, or assert worldwide public-domain status.

For the two newly selected songs, the reproduction basis is the source publishers' explicit public-domain identification, rather than an inferred historical publication date. Stanford DREME labels the B-I-N-G-O lyrics public domain. LowGUkulele.com identifies its freely arranged songs as public domain, labels John Jacob traditional, and states no exception for this song. Only the traditional lyric underlay is transcribed; the modern ukulele arrangement is separately licensed CC BY-NC 4.0 and is not reproduced. Details and exact versions appear below.

Modern source-site editorial prose, arrangements, audio, and artwork are not reproduced in the song sheets. The retained scans and source transcriptions below provide version-specific evidence; the repo's general software license does not alter the underlying songs' public-domain status.

### Row Your Boat

- [Full 1881 scan, Internet Archive](https://archive.org/details/franklinsquares04mccagoog); [direct scan PDF](https://archive.org/download/franklinsquares04mccagoog/franklinsquares04mccagoog.pdf). Printed p. 69 is PDF page 74.
- [Morgan Library catalog](https://www.themorgan.org/music-manuscripts-and-printed-music/311191) independently identifies the 1881 Harper publication and p. 69's familiar tune and words.
- Retained [page image](source-pages/row-your-boat-1881-p69.png) was visually checked, including all four entry marks. [Bibliographic fields](franklin-bibliography.json) were captured from the archive item's metadata.

### My Bonnie and Scotland's Burning

- [University of Pennsylvania library transcription and scans](https://digital.library.upenn.edu/women/aitch/songs/songs.html): [My Bonnie](https://digital.library.upenn.edu/women/aitch/songs/songs.html#My), [Scotland's Burning](https://digital.library.upenn.edu/women/aitch/songs/songs.html#Scotland).
- Retained original page images: [p. 43](source-pages/my-bonnie-1915-p43.jpeg) and [p. 55](source-pages/scotlands-burning-1915-p55.jpeg). The library's **1915 title/copyright transcription** establishes the chosen edition; its [retained text excerpt](source-pages/golden-book-1915-title.txt) identifies the tenth edition and Chicago publisher. The separate title-page JPEG timed out, so no title-page scan is claimed. The website cover alone is not used as edition evidence.
- Both lyrics were checked against the printed music, not solely the web transcription. Syllabic music hyphens are joined into words; curly apostrophes are represented as straight apostrophes. Punctuation follows the score, with phrase-ending punctuation made readable in the lyric-only layout. No words, word order, or repetitions are changed.
- The optional B-word movement is an established teaching convention, corroborated by [Irish Girl Guides' Trefoil News, Autumn 2018](https://irishgirlguides.ie/wp-content/uploads/2018/09/Trefoil-News-Autumn-2018.pdf) and its [Online Scout Manager activity](https://www.onlinescoutmanager.co.uk/programme.php?action=view&id=57835). Its mechanics are paraphrased in one short leader note. Those modern lyrics are not the transcription source. The action applies to every B-word in the retained verses and chorus, including blow, blown, bed, and bro't.

### B-I-N-G-O

- [Stanford DREME's one-page guide](https://familymath.stanford.edu/app/uploads/2022/03/BINGO.pdf) prints the farmer/dog verse and each successive clap pattern. It explicitly states that the song lyrics are public domain. This declaration applies to the lyrics; the surrounding 2022 teaching guide has its own copyright.
- Downloaded and rendered the original PDF; visually compared its Song Lyrics panel with the handout. The [retained lyric/status excerpt](source-pages/bingo-stanford-lyrics.txt) includes the source URL and downloaded PDF hash. Modern artwork and teaching-guide prose are not retained or copied.
- Printed optional **(who)** becomes **who** on the sheet. The source's first clap pattern ends in a typographic **0**; corrected to the letter **O**, consistent with the song's spelling elsewhere. The three initial B-I-N-G-O patterns are separated into three lines, preserving their count.
- The sheet labels its printed verse as **Pass 1** and provides the complete six-pass chart. Each pattern takes the place of the three spelling lines; the farmer/dog opening and final name-o line stay in every pass. This is repeat notation, not omitted verses or a new variant.

### John Jacob

- [Publisher page and public-domain statement](https://lowgukulele.com/john-jacob-jingleheimer-schmidt-for-ukulele-free-tab/); [linked one-page score](https://lowgukulele.com/wp-content/uploads/2025/05/John-Jacob-Jingleheimer-Schmidt-Low-G-Ukulele-Level-1.pdf). The score labels the song **Traditional**, and the publisher identifies the songs underlying its free arrangements as public domain unless noted otherwise. No contrary note appears for John Jacob.
- Downloaded and rendered the original score; visually checked every lyric syllable. The [retained traditional lyric/status record](source-pages/john-jacob-traditional-lyrics.txt) includes both source URLs and the downloaded PDF hash. Syllabic hyphens within words are joined; the title's spelling normalizes the score's mixed Jingleheimer capitalization. All lyric words, order, and seven ending syllables are preserved.
- Preserve this source's **They say** and **Fa-la-la-la-la-la-la!**, rather than mixing in other common versions' **There goes** or **Da**. The publisher's editorial prose mentions Da syllables; the sheet follows the actual score's Fa-la underlay.
- The public-domain identification concerns the traditional song. The separate modern tablature, notation, chords, arrangement, and page design are not reproduced. The packet does not adopt the modern score's CC BY-NC license or claim that its arrangement is public domain.
- The publisher describes repeating more quietly and returning to full volume for the final syllables. The sheet gives that established convention in independently written directions. No fixed number of passes is invented.
- No pre-1931 source for this exact verse is claimed; an early newspaper mention of a song title would not establish the words of this version.

## Candidate decisions

- **Kookaburra**: excluded. [Current licensing information](https://www.easysong.com/search/songs/song-copyright-holder-information.aspx?s=1012401) identifies Marion Sinclair and Music Sales on behalf of Larrikin Music. [Federal Court of Australia discussion of the litigation](https://www.fedcourt.gov.au/digital-law-library/judges-speeches/speeches-former-judges/justice-edelman/edelman-j-20161019) corroborates the rights dispute. No verified public-domain edition or reproduction permission was obtained. No lyrics or replacement song were generated.
- **Bring Back My Bonnie to Me**, H. J. Fulmer, 1882: public-domain age, but rejected as the source for the familiar camp song. The [published sheet and transcription](https://www.sheetmusicsinger.com/bring-back-my-bonnie-to-me/) contain different verses and a different chorus. Its publication date cannot authenticate familiar modern lyrics. The complete 1915 version above was selected instead.
- **Home on the Range**: considered through [Lomax's 1910 collection](https://www.gutenberg.org/files/21300/21300-h/21300-h.htm) and [Library of Congress history](https://www.loc.gov/collections/songs-of-america/articles-and-essays/articles-about-songs/home-on-the-range/). The other choices provide a bounded set of shorter teaching songs without making a shortened composite of its many historical stanzas.

## Fidelity record

[source-transcriptions.json](source-transcriptions.json) contains the active songs' manually source-checked lyric text, exact block order, and separately authored flow/teaching directions. The builder compares each HTML lyric block against that record, then checks that the exported PDF contains each complete block in order. Automatic comparisons protect against later drift; visual comparisons with the source publications establish source fidelity. B-I-N-G-O also records every numbered repeat pattern and its repeat cue, checked in both HTML and PDF. Neither check relies on invented continuation verses.

Row's optional two-group round uses the source's entry interval. Its directions are now limited to one short entry cue; the original longer ending explanation has been removed. No key or fixed tempo is imposed. The source scores retained here are available for leaders who need the melody.
