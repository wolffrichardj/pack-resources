Pack Operations Reference

Blue & Gold

Use this guide to run a repeatable Blue & Gold celebration with clear planning for food, recognition, gifts, supplies, photos, and cleanup.

Best For

  • Celebration chairs
  • AOL recognition planning
  • Annual handoff notes
Core Goal Make Blue & Gold feel celebratory and organized while ensuring recognition items, food, and event details are handled early enough to avoid last-minute scrambling.

Verification Lens

Another organizer should be able to see what was ordered, which decisions were pack-funded, how the gift plan worked, and what should be repeated or changed next year.

Pre-Event Planning

  • Confirm the event basics. Lock the date, venue, food plan, headcount, and recognition list.
  • Set gift expectations early. Decide who receives gifts or keepsakes and which costs belong to the pack, families, or a sponsor.
  • Start the shopping list early. Order any personalized items soon enough for proofing, shipping, and corrections.
  • Plan the room experience. Decide where food, cake, gifts, awards, and photos will happen so the room works for both celebration and recognition.

Food and Supplies

  • Food plan. Decide whether the meal is centrally ordered, potluck-based, or split across volunteers.
  • Serving supplies. Plates, cups, napkins, utensils, serving tools, and table coverings should follow the menu decision.
  • Cake plan. Assign who orders it, who picks it up, and what personalization details are required.
  • Decor and staging. Prepare table decor, gift tables, and any signage or labels that keep recognition orderly.

Recommended Event Flow

  1. Set up before families arrive.
    Stage food, cake, decorations, recognition items, and any photo area before the room fills.
  2. Welcome and gather the pack.
    Open with a brief framing of the celebration and any special milestones being recognized.
  3. Serve the shared meal or refreshments.
    Keep the food service aligned with the recognition timeline so the event does not stall out.
  4. Handle recognition cleanly.
    Present gifts or special recognition in a way that keeps names, items, and photos organized.
  5. Capture final notes and close out.
    Pack up leftovers, document what worked, and save vendor or ordering details for next year.

Roles and Responsibilities

  • Event lead: owns the timeline and cross-checks food, recognition, and room setup.
  • Food lead: manages ordering, pickups, serving supplies, and volunteers.
  • Recognition lead: prepares gifts, labels, and the order of presentation.
  • Photo helper: captures family or recognition photos as needed.

Cleanup and Closeout

  • Pack leftover decor, serving supplies, and reusable celebration materials together.
  • Save gift vendor details, food sources, personalization patterns, and final costs.
  • Write down what families liked, what ordering took too long, and what should be simplified next year.

Optional Example Notes

Cake Example

One prior pack recommended ordering online from Sam's Club because it was reliable and cost-effective, while also noting that other warehouse bakeries might work if your local logistics differ.

Recognition Gift Example

A prior event used personalized Arrow of Light plaques and Warrior Arrows, with careful proofing because custom orders could not be returned. The reusable lesson is to confirm names, dates, symbols, and funding responsibilities before placing the order.

Food Memory Example

One reimbursement history included pizza, cake, fruit, vegetables, tablecloths, and similar supplies. That kind of spend summary is useful to save for future budget planning even if the exact menu changes.
