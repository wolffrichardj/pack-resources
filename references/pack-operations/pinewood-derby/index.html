Pack Operations Reference

Pinewood Derby

Use this guide to run a repeatable Pinewood Derby with clear prep, check-in, race flow, awards, and teardown expectations.

Best For

  • Pack event chairs
  • Race-day volunteers
  • Future year handoffs
Core Goal Run a derby that is fair, easy to follow, and smooth for families, while leaving behind enough notes that another pack can repeat the event next year.

Verification Lens

After the event, a future organizer should be able to answer: what was needed, who handled it, what equipment was missing, and what should change next time.

Pre-Event Planning

  • Confirm the event basics. Lock the date, location, room layout, setup window, and teardown window.
  • Define the volunteer plan. Assign setup, check-in, race operations, runners, awards handling, and teardown.
  • Decide on race structure early. Choose how dens will race, how finalists are selected, and how any open or family race fits in.
  • Communicate family expectations. Share check-in rules, timing, race flow, and any optional extra race categories in advance.

Supplies and Equipment

  • Track and scoring setup. Track, start gate, scoring computer, cables, display, projector, and adapters.
  • Check-in station. Scale, dimension-check tools, labels or racer list, pens, and check-in table supplies.
  • Room setup. Tables, chairs, extension cords, tape, and crowd-control materials near the track.
  • Awards and recognition. Trophies, certificates, labels, and a plan for handing them out before the room loses energy.

Recommended Race Flow

  1. Set up the room and test the race system.
    Build the track, test the scoring connection, confirm the display works, and block off the track area before families arrive.
  2. Run check-in with consistent standards.
    Inspect weight and dimensions, label cars cleanly, and route families to the correct staging area.
  3. Keep heats moving.
    Use a race format that gives each car a fair number of runs and keeps downtime low for scouts and spectators.
  4. Recognize winners before energy drops.
    Present den-level or category awards while the event is still moving rather than saving everything for the end.
  5. Close with a clear final round and teardown.
    Run the final championship format, announce results, and transition directly into cleanup responsibilities.

Roles and Responsibilities

  • Event lead: oversees schedule, volunteers, and final decisions.
  • Check-in team: verifies cars and moves racers into the event queue.
  • Race operator: runs the software and race hardware.
  • Track marshal: keeps the race area clear and scouts moving safely.
  • Awards lead: prepares trophies or certificates and handles presentation timing.

Cleanup and Closeout

  • Pack the track, cables, projector, computer, scale, and check-in tools together.
  • Break down barriers, decorations, tables, and chairs.
  • Record missing equipment, damaged gear, and anything that slowed down check-in or racing.
  • Save next-year notes while details are still fresh, especially around fairness, timing, and supplies.

Optional Example Notes

Sample Race-Day Schedule

A prior pack schedule ran rank blocks in roughly 30-minute increments, then used a final round for top finishers. Treat that as a starting example only and recalculate based on pack size and track throughput.

Equipment Lessons Learned

One prior setup specifically noted the need for a new scale, a USB-C hub for the scoring computer, and a better barrier solution around the track. These are strong examples of the kind of notes worth preserving after each derby.

Open or Outlaw Race Option

Another pack used an optional open race for parents, grandparents, siblings, troop members, or scouts with older cars. If you adopt that idea, communicate the rules and registration process before race day.
